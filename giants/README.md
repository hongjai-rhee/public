# Giants

> **“If I have seen further, it is by standing on the shoulders of giants.”**
> — Isaac Newton, 1675

This book was not written alone.

Every model, algorithm, framework, and idea presented in this book stands on the work of people who came before us. Their ideas transformed the way we understand learning, intelligence, images, language, and decision-making.

I created this page to acknowledge some of those **giants**.

They are not merely names in references. Their ideas made this book possible.

---

## 🏛️ Richard Bellman — Seeing the Future Through the Present

**Contribution:** Dynamic Programming, Bellman Equation

Richard Bellman introduced dynamic programming and the **principle of optimality**, providing the conceptual foundation for modern reinforcement learning.

The Bellman equation taught us to look beyond the immediate reward:

> What matters now depends on what may happen next.

This simple but profound idea became one of the foundations on which modern reinforcement learning was built.

---

## 🧠 Yann LeCun — Teaching Machines to See

**Contribution:** Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs)

Yann LeCun and his collaborators demonstrated how neural networks could learn visual representations through convolutional architectures.

The idea that a machine can learn increasingly meaningful visual features—from edges to shapes to objects—became fundamental to modern computer vision.

CNNs became one of the central architectures through which machines learned to see.

---

## 🔥 Geoffrey Hinton, Yann LeCun & Yoshua Bengio — The Deep Learning Revolution

**Contribution:** Foundations of modern deep learning

Geoffrey Hinton, Yann LeCun, and Yoshua Bengio played pivotal roles in reviving and advancing neural-network-based learning.

Their work helped establish deep neural networks as a practical and powerful approach to machine learning.

The modern AI revolution owes an enormous debt to their persistence, ideas, experiments, and students.

---

## ⚙️ The TensorFlow Team — Making Deep Learning Practical

**Contribution:** TensorFlow

TensorFlow transformed deep learning from something that was difficult to implement at scale into a framework that students, researchers, and practitioners could experiment with.

The TensorFlow paper lists a large team of contributors, including Martín Abadi, Paul Barham, Jianmin Chen, Jeffrey Dean, Rajat Monga, Vijay Vasudevan, Pete Warden, Yuan Yu, and many others.

This book benefited enormously from their work.

Without frameworks such as TensorFlow, much of the experimentation in this book would have remained considerably harder.

---

## 🔭 Ashish Vaswani, Noam Shazeer, Niki Parmar, Jakob Uszkoreit, Llion Jones, Aidan N. Gomez, Łukasz Kaiser & Illia Polosukhin

**Contribution:** Transformer

In 2017, these eight researchers introduced the Transformer architecture in their landmark paper:

**“Attention Is All You Need.”**

The Transformer replaced recurrence with attention as the central mechanism for sequence modeling.

That seemingly simple shift changed the trajectory of natural language processing—and ultimately much of modern AI.

The idea behind self-attention also inspired one of the most important questions explored in this book:

> **How can a model decide what matters by looking at everything in relation to everything else?**

---

## 🤖 John Schulman, Filip Wolski, Prafulla Dhariwal, Alec Radford & Oleg Klimov

**Contribution:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)

Their 2017 paper introduced **Proximal Policy Optimization**, a remarkably practical approach to reinforcement learning.

PPO made it possible to teach an agent through repeated interaction:

**observe → act → receive reward → estimate advantage → update the policy**

The simplicity and effectiveness of this idea made PPO one of the most widely used reinforcement-learning algorithms.

## 🌱 And Many More Giants

The people listed here are only a small part of a much larger intellectual landscape.

Behind every algorithm are earlier ideas.

Behind every paper are teachers, colleagues, students, engineers, reviewers, and communities.

Behind every framework are thousands of contributors.

And behind every breakthrough are generations of people who asked questions that seemed impossible at the time.

So this directory is intentionally incomplete.

It is a reminder rather than a complete history.

---

## 🤝 And One More Giant — AI Itself

There is one final contributor I would like to acknowledge.

**Artificial intelligence itself.**

While writing this book, I had the opportunity to discuss ideas, explanations, examples, code, algorithms, and visualizations with **ChatGPT**.

It helped me turn many abstract ideas into explanations and experiments that could be shared with students.

I therefore include it here—not as the inventor of the ideas presented in this book, but as a **collaborative tool that helped me learn, organize, question, explain, and create.**

The ideas belong to the researchers and communities who developed them.

The responsibility for how they are presented in this book belongs to me.

And the conversation between human and machine belongs to our time.
